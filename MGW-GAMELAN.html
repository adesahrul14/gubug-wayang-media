GAMELAN

GAMELAN Tradisi Jawa meyakini bahwa Gamelan merupakan produk para dewa, terutama Hyang Pramesti Guru, dan disempurnakan oleh Sang Hyang Surapati (Indra). Bahkan ketika bidadari tercipta, sebuah tarian penuh keanggunan yang bernama tari Bedhaya juga dicipta oleh para dewa. Setelah gamelan diturunkan di dunia, Sri Maharaja Kano sebagai pewaris, melestarikan dan mengembangkan gamelan dengan berbagai komposisi alat musik dan penyempurnaan penggunaan gamelan dalam upacara kebesaran negara. Ketika tahun 544 tahun matahari, atau 560 tahun bulan, Prabu Basukesthi raja di Wiratha berinisiatif membuat tabuhan rebana, dengan mencontoh alat musik yang selalu digunakan oleh para resi. Maka, para resilah yang bertanggung jawab dalam pembuatan tabuhan rebana tersebut, dengan alat musik : • Bendhe; • Gentha; • Kekeleng; • Trebang; • Kenthongan; • Angklung (dalam tradisi lisan, masa Basukethi lah dimulainya tradisi musik angklung) Ketika tahun 1086, dengan ditandai sengkala berbunyi “Angraras Sarira Barakaning Dewa”, Prabu Jayalengkara dari negara Purwacarita (buyut dari Raden Panji Inukertapati), menambahkan alat musik gamelan yang berlaras slendro dengan seperangkat alat Gender (sepuluh bilah); Demung (lima bilah); Saron (lima bilah). Ketika sudah ada Gender maka gendhing-gendhing Abusinta diterapkan dalam alat musik tersebut. Dengan demikian musik tersebut, lebih dikenal dengan sebutan Gendhing Gender. Prabu Jayalengkaralah yang menyempurnakan ricikan gamelan hingga menjadi komplit hingga seperti sekarang ini. Bahkan diyakini, awal mula Laras Pelog juga dicipta jaman Prabu Jayalengkara. Dalam sumber litearasi lain, ketika negara Jenggala diperintah oleh Prabu Lembu Amiluhur, para putra mahkota di didik untuk mencintai tentang gamelan. Maka, Raden Panji Inukertapatilah yang merubah jumlah ricikan gamelan, menjadi: 1. Bonang Ageng; 2. Bonang Penerus; 3. Gender Penerus; 4. Demung; 5. Saron Penerus; 6. Ketipung; 7. Kecer; 8. Gambang Salukat; 9. Serta berkenan membuat gamelan berlaras slendro. Keluarga dari Kerajaan Jenggala juga dikenal sebagai pembuat Gamelan yang piawai. Raden Panji sendirilah yang memimpin pembuatan gamelan dengan memegang cupit (palu kecil), sedangkan yang melayani adalah para Kadeyan, yakni: Kalang, Andaga, Wirun. Raden Panimbal bertugas memukul bakalan gangsa. Panji Carangwaspa bertugas mematar gangsa, sedangkan yang menghalang-halangi percikan api adalah Dewi Ragil Kuning. Sedangkan yang memompa api (ngububi geni) adalah abdi Bancak dan Doyok. Sebul dan Pelet bertugas membersihkan calon gangsa dari debu-debu abu yang bertebangan. Semua dikerjakan di Besalen Gangsa. Maka sempurnalah ricikan gamelan: 1. Rebab; 2. Bonang Ageng (berjumlah 10 biji satu pangkon); 3. Bonang Penerus (berjumlah 10 biji satu pangkon); 4. Gender Ageng (berjumlah 11 biji satu pangkon); 5. Gender Penerus (berjumlah 11 biji satu pangkon); 6. Gambang (berjumlah 15 biji satu pangkon); 7. Demung Ageng Pencon (berjumlah 5 biji satu pangkon); 8. Demung (berjumlah 5 biji satu pangkon); 9. Saron Barung (berjumlah 5 biji satu pangkon); 10. Saron Penerus (berjumlah 5 biji satu pangkon); 11. Kenong (berjumlah 2 biji satu pangkon, dengan laras nem dan lima); 12. Kethuk (berjumlah 1 biji satu pangkon, dengan laras gulu [2]); 13. Gong (berjumlah 2 biji satu pangkon, dengan laras gulu [2] dan barang [1]); 14. Kempul berjumlah 2 biji satu pangkon, dengan laras nem dan 5); 15. Suling (berjumlah 1 biji satu pangkon); 16. Kendhang (berjumlah 1 biji satu pangkon); 17. Ketipung (berjumlah 1 biji satu pangkon); 18. Calempung (berjumlah 1 biji satu pangkon); 19. Kecer.

According to Javanese tradition, the gamelan is believed to have originated from the gods, particularly Hyang Pramesti Guru, and was later perfected by Sang Hyang Surapati (Indra). Furthermore, when the celestial nymphs were created, the gods also created an elegant dance known as the Bedhaya dance. After the gamelan was introduced to the world, Sri Maharaja Kano, as its successor, preserved and developed the gamelan by introducing various musical Instrument arrangements and refining its use in state ceremonies. In the year 544 of the solar calendar, or 560 of the lunar calendar, Prabu Basukesthi, the king of Wiratha, initiated the creation of tabuhan rebana (frame-drum musical ensembles) by drawing inspiration from musical instruments commonly used by the sages (resi). The sages were therefore responsible for producing the following instruments: - Bendhe; - Gentha; - Kekeleng; - Trebang; - Kenthongan; - Angklung (according to oral tradition, the angklung musical tradition originated during the reign of Basukesthi). In 1086, as indicated by the chronological inscription (sengkala) reading “Angraras Sarira Barakaning Dewa,” Prabu Jayalengkara of Purwacarita (the great-grandfather of Raden Panji Inukertapati) introduced additional instruments to the slendro-tuned gamelan ensemble. These instruments consisted of a Gender (ten metal bars), a Demung (five metal bars), and a Saron (five metal bars). Following the introduction of the Gender, the Abusinta compositions were performed using this Instrument. Consequently, this musical tradition became known as Gendhing Gender. Prabu Jayalengkara was credited with perfecting the gamelan instruments (ricikan) until the ensemble reached a complete arrangement resembling its present form. He was also believed to have introduced the pelog tuning system during his reign. According to another literary source, when the Kingdom of Jenggala was ruled by Prabu Lembu Amiluhur, the crown princes were educated to appreciate and develop gamelan music. Among them, Raden Panji Inukertapati reorganized the number and arrangement of gamelan instruments as follows: 1. Bonang Ageng; 2. Bonang Penerus; 3. Gender Penerus; 4. Demung; 5. Saron Penerus; 6. Ketipung; 7. Kecer; 8. Gambang Salukat; 9. He also contributed to the development of a slendro-tuned gamelan ensemble. The royal family of Jenggala was also renowned for its expertise in gamelan craftsmanship. Raden Panji personally supervised the gamelan-making process while holding a cupit (small hammer). He was assisted by the Kadeyan, namely Kalang, Andaga, and Wirun. Raden Panimbal was responsible for striking the initial bronze materials (bakalan gangsa), while Panji Carangwaspa shaped the bronze through hammering (mematar gangsa). Dewi Ragil Kuning was responsible for preventing sparks from scattering, while the servants Bancak and Doyok operated the bellows to maintain the fire (ngububi geni). Sebul and Pelet were assigned to clean the unfinished bronze instruments of the ash and dust produced during the process. All these activities took place in the Besalen Gangsa, a traditional bronze-working workshop. Through this process, the gamelan ensemble was considered complete, consisting of the following instruments: 1. Rebab; 2. Bonang Ageng, consisting of ten individual instruments (pencon) in one set; 3. Bonang Penerus, consisting of ten individual instruments (pencon) in one set; 4. Gender Ageng, consisting of eleven metal bars in one set; 5. Gender Penerus, consisting of eleven metal bars in one set; 6. Gambang, consisting of fifteen wooden bars in one set; 7. Demung Ageng Pencon, consisting of five individual instruments (pencon) in one set; 8. Demung, consisting of five individual instruments (pencon) in one set; 9. Saron Barung, consisting of five metal bars in one set; 10. Saron Penerus, consisting of five metal bars in one set; 11. Kenong, consisting of two individual instruments (pencon) in one set, tuned to nem and lima; 12. Kethuk, consisting of one individual instrument (pencon) in one set, tuned to gulu (2); 13. Gong, consisting of two individual instruments (pencon) in one set, tuned to gulu (2) and barang (1); 14. Kempul, consisting of two individual instruments (pencon) in one set, tuned to nem and lima (5); 15. Suling, consisting of one instrument in one set; 16. Kendhang, consisting of one instrument in one set; 17. Ketipung, consisting of one instrument in one set; 18. Calempung, consisting of one instrument in one set; 19. Kecer. <\p>
